# CASE STUDY
## FROM DECOMMISSIONING TO 12 MORE YEARS OF SERVICE

**Asset Recovery · Technical Leadership · Crew Mobilisation · Operational Continuity**

### 1. THE SITUATION

A port tug built in 1972 had become immobilised and was being considered for decommissioning.

The vessel performed two important functions for the port:
- Ship manoeuvring and assistance during vessel handling.
- Supplying fuel and water to visiting vessels.

Its continued operation therefore had direct operational and commercial value.

However, several critical systems were out of service:
- Main engines.
- Auxiliary engines.
- Air compressor.
- Fire pump.
- Ballast pump.

The situation was further complicated by low crew morale. Personnel were receiving only half their salaries and expected the vessel to be taken out of service.

### 2. THE CHALLENGE

The task was not simply to repair individual pieces of machinery.

The wider challenge was to restore the vessel as an operational asset under severe resource constraints, while rebuilding the team's confidence and willingness to work towards a common objective.

The crew needed coordination, a realistic recovery direction and access to spare parts that were difficult to obtain.

### 3. THE APPROACH

As Senior Mechanic, I brought the crew together around a shared objective: restoring the vessel and protecting its future.

I emphasised that the crew's collective effort would determine both the vessel's future and their own professional and financial prospects.

The recovery effort involved:

**Team mobilisation**

Re-established a shared sense of responsibility and coordinated the crew around the restoration objective.

**Technical recovery coordination**

Organised the work required to address failures affecting the vessel's main and auxiliary machinery and essential support systems.

**Resourcefulness under constraints**

Used professional contacts and established relationships to locate scarce spare parts. Some components were recovered from decommissioned vessels.

**Operational readiness**

Focused on restoring the vessel's ability to perform its intended port duties, rather than treating individual repairs as isolated achievements.

### 4. VERIFICATION AND RETURN TO SERVICE

After the restoration work, I declared the tug ready for operation and proposed that it be retained on the port's balance sheet.

Management initially viewed the proposal with scepticism.

Sea trials subsequently demonstrated that the vessel was operational. It was retained on the port's balance sheet and returned to service.

### 5. THE RESULT

The tug continued operating for another **12 years** after its restoration.

During this period, it continued performing ship manoeuvring and fuel and water supply duties for the port.

The result was not merely the recovery of machinery. It was the restoration of a productive port asset and the continuation of its operational and commercial contribution.

### 6. WHAT THIS EXPERIENCE DEMONSTRATES

This case illustrates several capabilities developed through practical operational experience:

- **Asset recovery:** restoring operational capability in an ageing asset.
- **Technical coordination:** addressing failures across multiple critical systems.
- **Leadership under pressure:** mobilising a team facing uncertainty and low morale.
- **Resourcefulness:** securing scarce components through professional networks and alternative sources.
- **Operational judgement:** focusing on the readiness and actual function of the whole vessel.
- **Long-term value:** returning an asset to service for a further 12 years.

### 7. PROFESSIONAL PERSPECTIVE

The central lesson is that an asset's condition cannot always be judged solely by its current operational state.

A vessel that appears to have reached the end of its service life may still have recoverable value when technical problems, resources, people and operational priorities are addressed together.

Effective operational leadership requires more than identifying what has failed. It requires organising the conditions under which recovery becomes possible.
